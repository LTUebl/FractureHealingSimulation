1. Biophysical Mechanoregulation Stimulus (Prendergast et al. 1997; Lacroix & Prendergast 2002)

Bone healing depends on how mechanical loads (patient activity) and implant stiffness translate into local tissue distortion and interstitial fluid motion inside the fracture callus. At each point in the biphasic poroelastic callus, the mechanoregulation stimulus \(S\) combines octahedral shear strain \(\gamma\) and relative fluid/solid velocity \(v\):

\[S = \frac{\gamma}{a} + \frac{v}{b}, \qquad a = 0.0375\ (3.75\%\text{ strain}), \quad b = 3\ \mu\text{m/s}\]

Depending on \(S\), progenitor mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) differentiate along distinct lineages: fibrous tissue (\(S > 3\)), cartilage (\(1 < S \le 3\)), immature woven bone (\(0.53 < S \le 1\)), mature bone (\(0.01 < S \le 0.53\)), or osteoclastic bone resorption (\(S \le 0.01\)).

2. Lattice Cell Activity & Material Evolution (Byrne et al. 2011, Table 1 & 2)

Precursor MSCs originate from the periosteum, endosteum, and bone marrow cavity. Motile cells (MSCs and fibroblasts) disperse via a nearest-neighbor random walk at \(30\ \mu\text{m/h}\). An MSC must reach a critical age of > 6 days before differentiating (rate \(0.3/\text{day}\)). Differentiated cells proliferate only within their favorable \(S\) band and undergo apoptosis at \(15\%/\text{day}\) outside it. Local element Young's modulus \(E\) is averaged across the last 10 iterations (rule of mixtures + 30-day synthesis curve) and woven bone adapts toward lamellar/cortical bone (\(17{,}000\text{ MPa}\)).

4. Large Defects, Fixation Stiffness & Bone Graft Stimulation (Perier-Metz et al. 2020)

In large segmental defects, unstimulated endogenous progenitor cells cease migrating/proliferating after a ~15-day latency period, causing marrow cavity capping and non-union. Adding an autologous bone graft or 3D osteoconductive scaffold restores healing via three mechanisms: (1) osteoconduction (guiding MSC migration/proliferation across the defect), (2) surface-guided ECM deposition along scaffold struts, and (3) osteoinduction (increasing osteogenic differentiation from \(0.3 \to 0.5/\text{day}\)). Rigid medial locking plates also introduce asymmetric strain shielding directly under the plate compared to the far cortex.
